📊 EMI Formula — How EMI is Calculated

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is calculated using the standard reducing balance method. Here is the exact formula used by all banks and financial institutions in India:

EMI = P × R × (1+R)^N / [(1+R)^N - 1] Where: P = Principal Loan Amount R = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) N = Loan Tenure in Months Example: ₹25,00,000 loan at 8.5% for 20 years R = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083 N = 20 × 12 = 240 months EMI = ₹25,00,000 × 0.007083 × (1.007083)^240 / [(1.007083)^240 - 1] EMI = ₹21,730 per month

Tips to Reduce Your EMI

  • Make a higher down payment. A larger down payment reduces the principal amount and directly reduces your monthly EMI.
  • Choose a longer tenure. Spreading repayment over more years reduces the monthly amount — but increases total interest paid.
  • Negotiate the interest rate. Maintaining a CIBIL score above 750 gives you bargaining power to get lower interest rates.
  • Make annual prepayments. Even small yearly lump sum payments reduce the outstanding principal and save significant interest over the loan tenure.
  • Compare multiple lenders. Even a 0.5% difference in interest rate saves lakhs over a 20-year home loan tenure.
  • Opt for balance transfer. If current rates are lower than your existing loan rate, transferring to a cheaper lender saves substantial interest.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI and how does it work?
EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — a fixed payment made to a lender every month. Each EMI payment includes two components: a portion goes toward repaying the principal loan amount, and the rest covers the interest charged on the outstanding balance. In the early months, a larger share goes toward interest; over time, the principal portion increases as the outstanding balance reduces.
How accurate is this EMI calculator?
This calculator uses the exact same reducing balance formula used by all Indian banks and RBI-regulated financial institutions. The results are accurate to the rupee for standard loan scenarios. Note that actual bank EMIs may vary slightly due to rounding conventions, processing fees or GST on fees — these can be accounted for using the Advanced Options section.
What is a good CIBIL score to get a home loan?
A CIBIL score of 750 or above is considered excellent and typically qualifies you for the best available interest rates. A score between 700–750 is good and most major banks will approve your loan. Below 700, you may face higher interest rates or rejection. Always check your CIBIL score before applying for any loan.
Should I choose a shorter or longer loan tenure?
A shorter tenure means higher monthly EMI but significantly less total interest paid — saving lakhs over the loan period. A longer tenure reduces monthly EMI burden but costs more in total interest. The ideal approach: choose the maximum affordable EMI (not more than 40–50% of monthly income) to minimise the total interest outgo.
How much home loan can I get on my salary?
Most banks use the FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) — typically 40–50% of your net monthly income can go toward EMI payments. For example, if your net salary is ₹80,000 per month, your maximum EMI eligibility is approximately ₹32,000–40,000 per month. At 8.5% interest for 20 years, this translates to a home loan of approximately ₹37–46 Lakh.
