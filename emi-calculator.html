🏦 What is EMI and How is it Calculated?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay every month to your bank or lender to repay a loan. Every EMI payment consists of two components — a portion that repays the principal (the actual loan amount) and a portion that pays the interest charged by the bank.

In the early months of a loan, a larger portion of your EMI goes toward interest and a smaller portion toward principal. As the loan matures, this ratio gradually reverses — more of your EMI reduces the principal. This is called an amortizing loan, and it is the standard structure for all home loans, car loans and personal loans in India.

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]
P = Principal Loan Amount  |  r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
n = Loan Tenure in Months (Years × 12)

EMI Calculation Example

For a ₹50 Lakh home loan at 8.5% p.a. for 20 years:

r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083  |  n = 20 × 12 = 240 months

EMI = 50,00,000 × 0.007083 × (1.007083)²⁴⁰ ÷ [(1.007083)²⁴⁰ − 1] = ₹43,391 per month

📊 Home Loan vs Car Loan vs Personal Loan — Key Differences

🏠
Home Loan Longest tenure (up to 30 years), lowest interest rate (7.5%–9.5%), secured against property. Tax benefit available under Section 80C and Section 24(b). Best for large amounts ₹20L–₹5Cr.
🚗
Car Loan Medium tenure (1–7 years), interest rate 8%–12%, secured against vehicle. No tax benefit. Best for ₹3L–₹30L. The car serves as collateral so rates are lower than personal loans.
💼
Personal Loan Short tenure (1–5 years), highest interest rate (10%–24%), completely unsecured. No collateral needed. Best for ₹50K–₹25L. Fastest approval but most expensive loan type.
📉
How to Reduce EMI Pay higher down payment to reduce principal. Negotiate a lower interest rate. Choose longer tenure. Make part-prepayments when you have surplus funds to reduce outstanding principal.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EMI formula and how is it calculated? +
EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]

Where P = Principal loan amount, r = Monthly interest rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = Tenure in months.

Example: ₹10 Lakh loan, 10% annual interest, 5 years:
r = 10÷12÷100 = 0.00833 | n = 60
EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.00833 × (1.00833)⁶⁰ ÷ [(1.00833)⁶⁰−1] = ₹21,247/month
How can I reduce my loan EMI? +
There are four ways to reduce your EMI:

1. Increase tenure — Longer repayment period = lower monthly EMI (but higher total interest)
2. Reduce principal — Pay a larger down payment to borrow less
3. Negotiate lower rate — Even 0.5% reduction saves significant money; compare banks
4. Part prepayments — Making extra payments reduces outstanding principal, which reduces future EMI or shortens tenure

Use the calculator to simulate different combinations and find your optimal balance.
Is this EMI calculator accurate? +
Yes. This calculator uses the standard reducing-balance EMI formula used by all Indian banks and RBI-regulated lenders. Results match calculations from SBI, HDFC, ICICI and other major banks.

Note: This is a planning tool based on a fixed interest rate. Actual EMIs may vary slightly if your loan has a floating interest rate (linked to MCLR or repo rate) as rates change over the loan tenure. For the most accurate figures, always confirm with your bank's loan offer letter.
What is an amortization schedule? +
An amortization schedule is a complete table showing every payment over the life of a loan. It breaks down each year's payments into the principal component and interest component, and shows the outstanding loan balance at the end of each year.

This tool shows a year-wise amortization schedule — click "Show Table" in the results section. It clearly shows how the principal-to-interest ratio shifts over time. In the first year of a 20-year home loan, roughly 70–75% of your EMI is interest. By year 18, over 70% goes toward principal.
